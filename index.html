How CardioCheck Works

A 3-stage pipeline that automates the manual caliper measurement of the heart-to-chest ratio.

01

Chest X-ray Upload

A front-view chest X-ray (PNG or JPEG) is resized and normalized to the format the model expects. The image is processed in memory on the local server and is not stored.

check_circleAutomatic resize and normalization
02

U-Net Heart & Lung Segmentation

A pretrained U-Net generates pixel masks for the heart, the left lung and the right lung. The model also predicts the X-ray view (PA, AP or lateral).

check_circleView detection accuracy: 99.42%
03

Automated CTR & Stratification

The engine calculates transverse cardiac diameter (A+B) relative to maximal internal thoracic diameter (C). CTR values are classified into Normal, Borderline, or Cardiomegaly with immediate alert flags.

check_circleResults returned as JSON

The Model Behind CardioCheck

CardioCheck uses a pretrained U-Net with an EfficientNetV2-S encoder. It was trained on chest X-rays from the CheXpert and NIH Chest X-ray datasets, with heart and lung masks from the CheXmask dataset.

Understanding the Cardiothoracic Ratio (CTR)

The cardiothoracic ratio is a fundamental index used in diagnostic radiology to estimate heart enlargement on standard PA chest radiographs.

Standard Mathematical Definition

CTR = (Cardiac Diameter A + B) / (Thoracic Width C)
Measured as the widest left-to-right width of the heart divided by the widest internal width of the chest (inner edge of the ribs, usually just above the costophrenic angles), on a full-inspiration posteroanterior (PA) film. Supine or AP films tend to exaggerate the ratio.
CTR ≤ 0.50

Normal Cardiac Silhouette

Heart size is within the usual adult range on a PA film. A normal ratio does not rule out heart disease.

CTR 0.51 – 0.55

Borderline Enlargement

Marginal increase. Can be caused by poor inspiration, body build, or early heart changes. This band is a project convention, not a standard clinical category.

CTR > 0.55

Suspected Cardiomegaly

Above the usual 0.50 cutoff. Possible causes include true heart enlargement or pericardial effusion, but also poor inspiration, obesity, pregnancy, or an epicardial fat pad.

info

Limitations: This tool measures widths from AI-generated segmentation masks. Its ratio uses the width of both lungs, not the internal chest width, so it is an estimate of the standard clinical cardiothoracic ratio and not the same measurement. The 0.50 cutoff fits adult PA X-rays best. Children and AP images have different normal ranges, and expiration, obesity or pregnancy can raise the ratio in a normal heart. Image quality, positioning and segmentation errors can also change the result. The value cannot diagnose or rule out heart disease and must not be used for treatment decisions.
